## Course Overview

This course provides a comprehensive introduction to reservoir petrophysics. By the end of this course, students will be able to:

- Define porosity and the factors that influence it
- Describe laboratory and field methods for measuring it, and carry out porosity experiments
- Explain the electrical properties of reservoir rock (resistivity index, saturation exponent, cementation factor) and how they relate to one another and are used
- Measure electrical properties in the lab 
- Define permeability and what controls it
- Describe how permeability is measured and run experiments on hydraulic conductivity and on liquid and gas permeability
- Write Darcy's law in differential form, explain what it means, integrate it for typical reservoir systems, and account for the effect of fractures and channels
- Explain interfacial tension and wettability 
- Correlate interfacial tenstion and wettability with capillary pressure, describe how capillary pressure is measured, and convert laboratory values to reservoir conditions
- Describe how fluid saturations are determined and how they relate to capillary pressure
- Define effective and relative permeability, recognize typical relative permeability curves, and explain how saturation history affects them
- Describe the elastic and acoustic properties and strength of rocks
